# Expert elicitation for tacit organisational evidence

Use this optional reference inside `organisational-intelligence` when bounded
retrieval has left a **material** rationale, exception, practice, or competency
gap that a person may be able to clarify. It is evidence gathering, not a
separate knowledge-management, personnel-assessment, or memory-write workflow.
The owning skill's decision frame, claim-specific authority, privacy, and stop
conditions continue to apply.

## Decide whether to elicit

- **Do not interview** when current authoritative evidence already resolves the
  decision, or when the missing detail would not change its outcome.
- Identify the smallest unresolved claim, the decision it affects, and the
  evidence already inspected. Avoid a general "tell me everything you know"
  exercise.
- Find a potential respondent through attributable participation in relevant
  decisions, reviews, incidents, or operations. A frequently tagged name or
  high message count is a lead, not proof of expertise, availability, authority,
  or sole ownership.
- Establish an appropriate communication path and that the respondent is
  willing and permitted to discuss the subject. If no interview is possible or
  authorised, return a targeted question set for the requester; do not
  impersonate the respondent or contact them uninvited.
- Check the intended audience, source restrictions, and sensitivity *before*
  gathering testimony. Minimise personal data and do not turn the exercise
  into performance, personality, or employment profiling.

## Ask for situated examples, not polished generalities

Use a few focused, open questions, adapting to the decision and what is already
known. The aim is to discover where a rule came from and where its application
breaks down:

1. "What was the last concrete case where this constraint mattered? What
   happened, when, and what record or incident would show it?"
2. "What were the alternatives, what was rejected, and what evidence or failure
   made that choice reasonable at the time?"
3. "When does the usual approach not apply? Can you walk through an exception,
   including who was actually accountable?"
4. "What has changed since the original decision? Which parts have you observed
   yourself, and which were passed on to you?"
5. "If a new maintainer met a different case tomorrow, what would you want them
   to recognise, test, or escalate before acting?"

Ask only the subset needed. Follow unexpected details with a concrete
counterexample, an observable discriminator, or a precise source reference;
do not lead the respondent toward the model's preferred explanation. Distinguish
what they witnessed from hearsay, assumptions, present-day policy, and
reconstruction. Capture dates, scope, and exact handles when provided. Avoid
recording full transcripts or sensitive anecdotes by default.

## Corroborate and classify

- If a source record exists, inspect it and follow the original thread through
  corrections and reversals; avoid relying on a retelling alone.
- Attribute a human recollection only with appropriate consent and an accurate
  reference such as an authorised interview note, dated statement, or relevant
  existing thread. Never invent citations, permissions, or endorsements.
- Label unsupported testimony as **human-reported**, with who/when/scope only
  when disclosure is authorised. Mark an unrecorded or unshareable claim as a
  gap if it cannot be safely supported for the intended readers.
- Test consequential claims against an independent evidence class or another
  accountable source where feasible. A second person repeating the same story
  is not automatically independent corroboration.
- Show disagreements with canonical policy, runtime evidence, or other testimony;
  do not automatically promote recollection into current policy, ownership,
  accepted intent, or approval.
- Identify the smallest resolving check, experiment, or accountable decision
  when recollection and evidence remain incompatible.

## Deliver the minimum useful output

For a bounded investigation, provide only what the consumer needs:

- **Established**: source-backed findings with exact locators and applicable
  dates/scopes.
- **Human-reported**: attributable but not independently confirmed recollections,
  with their limits and any conflicting evidence.
- **Unresolved**: the exact missing fact, conflicting claims, or inaccessible
  source; who *might* clarify it, if appropriate, and the precise question.
- **Next action**: the smallest verification, decision, procedure correction, or
  handover step that could close the consequential gap.

If the outcome is a handover, describe operational warnings, exceptions, and
decision points in the order the successor will encounter them. When human
recovery competence materially matters, offer a representative scenario and
request an explain-back from the recipient; do not mark understanding verified
without observing their answer.

## Prepare for durable capture, without writing it

Only propose a capture candidate when a reusable finding passes the durability
test and its original owner/source can be identified or explicitly remains
unknown. Include:

- proposed stable topic and canonical owner/destination, if established;
- concise claim, epistemic status, source/date/validity, and unresolved conflicts;
- verification needed, intended readers, access restrictions, and review trigger.

Prefer correcting an authoritative runbook, policy, ADR, or other owning record.
A secondary shared-memory synopsis must remain subordinate to that source.
Do not create or update memory from this reference. Hand off only after separate
authorisation to a configured memory workflow that enforces effective readership
and verifies the persisted result. If access cannot be verified, do not disclose
restricted content, source metadata, or even the existence of sensitive sources
in a broader-reader handover.

## Stop conditions

Stop when the decision-relevant question is answered sufficiently, the
respondent declines or cannot be reached, elicitation adds no new discriminator,
the required evidence is inaccessible, or sensitive disclosure would be unsafe.
Return the exact uncertainty rather than requesting more interviews for
ceremony.
