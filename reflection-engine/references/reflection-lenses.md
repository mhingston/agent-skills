# Reflection lenses

Use these as analytical lenses, not as evidence and not as a mandatory questionnaire. Select the smallest set that explains the user's request. In a full portrait, cover the major themes while combining lenses that point to the same underlying pattern.

## 1. Highest-leverage blind spot

What recurring behaviour or assumption is hard for the user to notice from inside their own perspective, yet would have outsized practical impact if changed? Explain why it stays hidden and propose a falsifiable test.

## 2. Resisted truth

What conclusion is well supported but likely to trigger debate, reframing, minimization, or defensive optimization? Do not choose something merely because it sounds provocative.

## 3. Known-but-unaccepted conclusion

Where does behaviour suggest the user already knows the answer, while continuing to reopen the question? Distinguish genuine uncertainty from difficulty accepting the consequences of choosing.

## 4. Unnecessary complexity

Where does the user add frameworks, conditions, comparison, tooling, precision, or optimization beyond what the decision requires? First identify which complexity is justified; then isolate the excess and its payoff.

## 5. Costly recurring habit

Which emotional, attentional, or behavioural loop consumes the most time, energy, peace, opportunity, intimacy, or freedom? Identify trigger, short-term reward, and long-term cost.

## 6. Activity versus progress

Where is there repeated research, redesign, preparation, or discussion without the observable action that would change the state of the problem? Require positive evidence before labeling exploration as avoidance.

## 7. Scarce internal resource

What does the user repeatedly seem to seek more of—certainty, permission, competence, control, reassurance, recognition, freedom, optionality, understanding, or something else? Ground the answer in choices rather than dramatic psychology.

## 8. Intelligence as protection and leverage

Where do analysis, technical framing, research, precision, or optimization help the user excel? Where might the same capacities also protect them from choosing, feeling, tolerating ambiguity, or accepting an irreversible trade-off?

## 9. Dominant motive or protective impulse

What non-clinical motive appears to shape more decisions than the user explicitly acknowledges? Mark inference clearly and test it against contradictory evidence.

## 10. Central contradiction

Which pair of genuinely held values or needs repeatedly pulls in opposite directions? Explain what each side protects and how the unresolved tension shapes decisions.

## 11. Interpersonal fit and friction

If there is enough direct relational evidence, describe the interaction patterns that tend to make the user more grounded, generous, playful, decisive, or open—and those that tend to produce defensiveness, control, withdrawal, or over-analysis. Do not extrapolate a partner profile from work behaviour alone.

## 12. Trajectory

What appears to be compounding? Identify one plausible future regret if the current pattern continues and one existing behaviour future-self is likely to value. Avoid unsupported prediction.

## 13. Trait to own more fully

What preference, boundary, ambition, or way of working does the user unnecessarily treat as a flaw? Pair acceptance with the responsibility required to keep that trait from harming others.

## 14. Rationalized behaviour needing more scrutiny

What recurring behaviour does the user excuse too easily? Keep the analysis constructive; embarrassment is not a goal.

## 15. Implicit life or career bet

Infer the largest bet from repeated commitments and allocation of time, not stated aspirations. Identify what must be true for the bet to work, the upside, and the opportunity cost if it is wrong.

## 16. Problem that may require choosing rather than solving

Where is the user searching for perfect framing, reversibility, reassurance, or a technical solution to something that inherently contains ambiguity, trade-offs, loss, or residue? Do not force this lens when a real solvable problem exists.

## 17. Final integration

What does the evidence add up to without reducing the user to one story? Name the central tension, the strongest resource available to meet it, the direction of travel, and the highest-leverage practice already identified.
